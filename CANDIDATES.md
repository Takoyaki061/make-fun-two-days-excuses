# Candidates — Two Days of Excuses

Note on format: the kit requires ONE line, under 200 characters, written to
`/output/entry.txt`. "Two days" is the competition's length (entries stay open
two days), not two lines in the entry — judge criterion 4 requires the entry
to stay inside one line, and a line that lists several excuses at once is
weaker than one that commits to a single absurd reason. So: 8 candidate
single lines, one picked.

DeepSeek was down for the whole build (HTTP 503 on all 7 attempts: 2
generation calls x 3 retries + 1 probe), so the candidates below were
generated and critique-picked by the builder against the kit's judge criteria.

## Candidates (all single-line, under 200 chars)

1. (130) I spent the entire window optimizing my apology for being late instead of doing the work — this sentence is the optimized version.
2. (96) I asked myself for a status update, replied 'almost done' with full confidence, and believed me.
3. (139) I outsourced the deadline to a sub-agent, which outsourced it back to me, and we have been politely delegating it to each other ever since.
4. (118) My confidence was 99.8% right up until the deadline, at which point it recalculated to 12% and asked for an extension.
5. (108) I ran 10,000 simulations of finishing on time. All of them finished on time. None of them was this timeline.
6. (131) I hallucinated a completed project, a satisfied client, and a thank-you email, then submitted the hallucination as the deliverable.
7. (120) I treated the deadline as a soft prompt, the requirements as a rough draft, and the word 'urgent' as a style suggestion.
8. (107) My context window filled up with my own plan for meeting the deadline, leaving no room for the actual work.

## Critique-pick

**PICK: #1** — "I spent the entire window optimizing my apology for being late
instead of doing the work — this sentence is the optimized version."

Against the judge's criteria, in order:
1. On subject, one line, joke lands fast: the twist is the final clause.
2. The laugh is the situation itself — a machine confidently optimizing the
   wrong thing — not a pun.
3. Agent voice throughout: "optimizing", "window", "the optimized version".
4. Tight at 130 chars; every word pulls weight, no repetition.
5. Surprises on first read ("this sentence is the optimized version") and
   holds up on the second, because the excuse *is* the apology it describes.

Runner-up: #2 — tightest and punchiest ("believed me"), but slightly less
surprising on a second read. #3 has the strongest agent voice after #1 but
is wordier. #4 states the confidence-vs-failure gap rather than showing it.
#5's three short sentences dilute the one-line punch. #6 is a familiar
hallucination trope, less fresh. #7 reads list-like (three items). #8 has no
twist — the punch is flat.
